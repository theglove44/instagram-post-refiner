# Claude archive lessons

Derived from 145 Claude project conversations containing 632 messages, including 87 caption-producing chats and 148 caption variants. Give highest weight to captions Chris explicitly identified as final.

## Repeated feedback

### Synthesize, do not transcribe

Chris repeatedly rejected drafts that read like brain dumps, bullet points, or staccato lists. Select one story. Reorder notes. Combine related details. Add connective tissue grounded in supplied facts. Vary sentence length.

### Hooks need work

Hook feedback appeared throughout archive. Default to short question rooted in exact subject. Invite a genuine choice, opinion, memory, or recommendation. Do not explain whole post in hook. Cut trailing clauses when first question already works.

Good patterns:

- `Salt & pepper squid or a flat iron steak plate in the sun?`
- `Has Lidl just beaten Ben & Jerry's?`
- `First sunny day of the year, so where do you head?`
- `Raspberry sour or a New England IPA under the railway arches in Leeds?`

Avoid generic questions such as `Who loves good food?` and long questions containing setup plus payoff.

### Emoji carry personality

Chris repeatedly asked for more emoji when drafts looked flat. Use emoji to lift emotion, humour, food reaction, weather, drink, music, or visual moment. Spread them through caption. Avoid uniform one-per-paragraph machinery.

### Preserve natural phrases

Strong supplied phrases often deserve keeping:

- `enough buffalo sauce to not be embarrassed`
- `the highlights is the future`
- `we were allowed to stay out late`
- `bring your shades and your factor 50`
- `the little bits of cauliflower that fall off when you're cutting it up`

Build polished structure around these phrases. Do not polish away personality.

### Avoid alien voice

Chris rejected phrases such as `this place goes off`, `Reader, we cleared the lot`, unexplained influencer language, and vague claims. Use words Chris would say aloud.

### Honest criticism needs balance

Name concrete disappointment, value issue, or service gripe. Keep wider experience fair. Positive company, occasion, or atmosphere can coexist with weak food.

### Tommo provides proof and contrast

Use Tommo's real preference or reaction as evidence. `Even Tommo loved them, and she's not normally a wing fan` works because it changes meaning. Do not add Tommo mechanically.

### Formatting preferences

- Never use em dashes.
- Use exactly five hashtags with `#tuckinandtalk` first.
- Use selective CAPS for anchors.
- Use blank lines for mobile reading.
- Keep Reel first line at 55 characters or fewer as house rule.
- Do not open with emoji.
